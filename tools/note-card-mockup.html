The note caught mid-sitting

Three screen shapes from the table in CLAUDE.md, insets and all. The card is draggable. Pull it down and it follows your pointer exactly; push it up and it resists; let go past the threshold and it leaves on the momentum you gave it, keeping the note. The keyboard is the floor — the card slides down toward it, meets it, and goes behind it — which is why this page is drawn rather than run through npm run web: Keyboard is a stub on web, so the preview can only ever show the keyboard-down half. Today is what ships now: a single line that cannot wrap, and — with the keyboard up — a card underneath it, because edge-to-edge stopped adjustResize resizing anything. A browser is honest about the shape of a gesture and not about its amount; settle the threshold in millimetres of thumb on the phone.

The two shadows — the same drag, rendered twice

Left is the shadow the gesture wants: blur ramping as the card lifts, and getting lighter and wider on the way up, never darker. Right is the one React Native can actually drive — blur cannot be animated on the native driver, so it is one pre-blurred shade, faded and nudged. Drag either; both move together, so the only difference on screen is the rendering.

The right-hand shade's box is inset inside the card, and that is the whole trick rather than a detail. A box shadow is clipped to the outside of the box casting it — it is a ring around a hole — so scaling the element moves the ring outward instead of spreading it, and an offset opens a clean gap of exactly that offset between a card and its own shadow. This page had it at inset: 0 and scaled to 1.45, and full lift drew a hard-edged rounded rectangle 60pt out with bare paper between: a second, wrong-sized card. Set the box in by more than the shadow can reach and the bright edge hides under the paper.

Watch the rest frame hardest. This app had no shadows at all before this card, and the rest state is where that rule has to survive — at at: 0 the card is lying on the page and whatever is drawn must not be findable.

Readout
The threshold

A share of the card's own height, floored and capped. The floor is what the docked reader needs — a two-line note is a short card, and a share of it is a flick — and the cap is what the 3:4 card on a tall phone needs. One rule, two shapes: the same argument this page already makes about an iPhone SE deciding the width share.

The hand

Intent, not events. A pointer that stopped before it lifted has no throw in it, whatever the smoothed average still says — that is STALE_MS, and it is the difference between a card put down and a card thrown. PROJECTION_MS decides whether it leaves; THROW_DAMPING decides how.

The springs

Return and exit are chosen separately on purpose. Motion is asymmetric — a door thrown open rebounds off its own hinge and a door pushed shut meets a frame and stops dead — and collapsing the two to one preset is the mistake the source material warns about by name. Critical damping is 2·√(k·m); the character of a preset is entirely in how far below it the damping sits.

Shadow — what the native driver can carry

One shade baked at a fixed blur, inset inside the card, then faded and nudged. Opacity is what stands in for ink; scale stays near 1 and does the little spreading a rasterised blur gives it, because the inset is what keeps the box's own edge hidden and a large scale is what broke this in the first place. The bake cannot be at both ends at once — that is the whole of the compromise, and it is what the two frames above are for.

Keep SHADE_INSET above SHADE_BLUR. Reach is blur plus spread; below that line the rest state starts reaching past the card, which is the one thing it may never do. Measured rather than assumed: a 28pt blur stops reaching at an inset of 31, identically at every card shape and every opacity in the table.
